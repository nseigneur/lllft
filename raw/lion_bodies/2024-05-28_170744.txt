TITLE: The Tsalala Female Has Cubs!
DATE: 2024-05-28
URL: https://blog.londolozi.com/2024/05/28/the-tsalala-female-has-cubs/

We are overjoyed to share some heartwarming news about the Tsalala Female, a lioness who holds a special place in all our hearts. Since our last update on the Tsalala Female hoping for her to have cubs soon, she ended up mating with the Plains Camp Males again at the beginning of February, so as our calendars ticked through the middle of May, we anxiously awaited any news of the arrival of some new cubs. ON boulders Having become quite elusive of late the Tsalala Female still stuck to the fringes of the Sand River and had spent a significant amount of time west of our boundary. Very recently we got the exciting news of some pretty compelling evidence that she has given birth and is caring for her cubs in a rocky outcrop in the central parts of their reserve. Sightings of her staying in the same area on the koppies for several days, followed by sightings of her with fresh suckle marks, have given us strong reason to believe she has finally become a mother. plaque rock Although the cubs have yet to be seen, and we do not know their number, this development is a significant and hopeful step in the Tsalala Pride’s journey. The Tsalala Female has been dealt a difficult hand from the start, mirroring the challenges faced by her mother, who also raised her first litter alone after the other lionesses in the pride died. This lioness is now walking the same solitary path, showing the same resilience and determination that has come to define the Tsalala Lineage. river We are all rooting for her to succeed with this litter and help boost the numbers in the pride. Her journey into motherhood will be incredibly challenging, as she ventures into this new chapter without the support and guidance of other lionesses, who typically help care for and protect the cubs. look up The belief that the Plains Camp Males are the fathers, could explain her decision to establish a den in the core of their territory. Moving east would mean facing too much competition from the Ntsevu and Kambula Prides, and to the north lies the Nkuhuma Pride. We also know she did not mate with the Ndzhenga Males and Ntsevu/Kambula Males, which would pose a significant risk to her and her cubs. river This news brings a renewed sense of hope and excitement as we witness the Tsalala Pride slowly making a comeback. We are all deeply invested in the Tsalala Female’s story and will continue to follow her journey closely, hoping for the best for her and her new cubs.
